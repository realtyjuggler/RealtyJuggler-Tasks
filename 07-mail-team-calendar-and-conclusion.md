# Chapter 7: Mail, Team Tasks, Calendar and Conclusion

## Printed Letters and Failed Email

When you schedule a letter to be printed using RealtyJuggler, an Auto Task is created with the date the letter should be printed. From the Tasks list, click the task text for that row to go to the Mail section of the record that needs the letter printed. Click the "Download" link on that screen to download the letter(s) that need to be printed. Once the download is complete, the AutoTask for that letter goes away.

> Image: Screenshot of the Mail tab. Buttons read "Done", "New Mail" and "New Drip Mail". Below is a "Select multiple rows" link and a table with columns Date, To, "Drip Group - Subject" and Status. One row shows an "@" email icon, "Nov 30", "Craig Abbott", "Thank you letter" and the status "Pending". Beneath the table is a "Download a printable document of" link with a drop-down "Pending Letters Due within 24 Hours" and the text "that have Media of Print". A final line reads "Pending Mails with Media of EMail (@) are sent automatically."

If an email is not being sent, click the task to correct the problem. Possible issues include a contact with no valid email address or a contact who unsubscribed from your mailing. In either case, click the individual message and make a correction to get things going again. You may want to change the "Media:" to Print or fill in an email address. Make sure you reset the "Status:" to "Pending" so RealtyJuggler can take a fresh try at the message.

If you set the Status for a Mail message to "Draft", it is not sent, but a task is displayed for that letter. Some realtors like to use DRIP mail but proofread each letter before it goes out, and setting the status to Draft is perfect for that. You get the benefit of an automatic sequence of letters while keeping total control over when each letter is sent and whether it goes by email or printed letter.

## Assigning Tasks to Team Members

When you create a Task, one of the fields you can edit is the Categories field. It can be used for any purpose. If you work in a team, you can enter the name of the person assigned to handle the task.

> Image: Screenshot of the New Task form with buttons "Done" and "Delete Task". Fields are Task Description, Categories (a blue label with arrow), Due Date (today), Repeat (None), Email Alarm (None), Done, Date Completed (No Date) and Notes.

To add a Category, click the text field and type it in. You can assign as many categories to a single task as you like, with a comma between each category.

Make sure to enter your categories in the Main:Settings:Preferences:Tasks section of RealtyJuggler. Fill in the Categories section with one category per line. It is a text field, so you can click any line and press Enter to add your task categories. Once your categories are added, click the "Categories:" link (the small blue arrow) and a yellow drop-down menu appears. Select the category you want from the menu.

> Image: Screenshot of a Categories field with a yellow drop-down menu listing Anniversary, AutoTask, B-Day, "B-Day OR Anniver...", Blue, Closing and Commission.

Categories can be used to filter your tasks from the Main:All Tasks section by selecting from the "Show Category:" drop-down menu in the upper-right corner of the screen. This is particularly useful for team members who want to see all the tasks assigned to a particular team member.

> Image: Screenshot of a "Show Category:" drop-down set to "All", with a large black checkmark beside it.

Automatic Tasks have the category AutoTask. Many automatic tasks also have an additional category that lets you organize your Tasks logically. These categories cannot be edited and are built into RealtyJuggler.

You can use color names for Categories. The color names are blue, green, purple, red, yellow, orange, turquoise, gray, boldblue, boldgreen and boldred.

> Image: A list of the supported color names, each on a block of its own color: blue, green, purple, red, yellow, orange, turquoise, gray (also written "or grey"), boldblue, boldgreen and boldred.

## Adding Dates to Your Calendar

The dates throughout RealtyJuggler are editable by clicking the "No Date" link, or the date itself. A Pick Date window appears so you can select the date. If a time is available, you can select the time by clicking the time part of the field and entering it.

> Image: Screenshot of a date row. A drop-down "Other Date" is followed by a blue down arrow and the "No Date" link. A yellow drop-down menu is open listing "LookUp Appointment", "Add to Calendar", "View Graphical Calendar" and "Help". Below, a "B-Day" drop-down and a "Google Drive Folder" drop-down are visible.

> Image: Screenshot of the "Pick Date" calendar pop-up for November 2016, with month and year drop-downs, arrow buttons, a grid of days with the 29th highlighted, and "Today", "No Date" and "Cancel" buttons.

Most dates in RealtyJuggler have a blue down arrow next to the date name. Click the date name or the blue arrow and a drop-down menu appears. Select "Add to Calendar" to add the date (and time, if available) to your calendar as an appointment.

> Image: Screenshot of the Contingency form with buttons "Done" and "Delete Contingency", fields Description, Status (Unknown), Deadline (No Date) and a Notes box.

If you select this option, the date is no longer editable and a blue double arrow >> appears to the right of the date. Click it to go directly to the Appointment record that appears in your calendar. From there you can add notes, assign categories and fill in the Location of the appointment. Click "Done" and you return to where you were before.

> Image: Screenshot of a date row reading "Deadline: Nov 19, 2009" followed by a blue >> arrow, with a "Document:" checkbox beneath it.

Many dates entered in RealtyJuggler are used automatically within Automatic Tasks. For example, if you have an incomplete contingency, the Deadline date is used for the Contingency in your Tasks tab.

If you have a date in RealtyJuggler you want on your Calendar, click the blue down arrow and select the "Add to Calendar" menu item.

## Alarms – Tasks vs. Appointments

Appointments normally have a date and a time associated with them. If you miss an appointment, you have missed it. There is no point showing up for an appointment 5 hours late, because no one will be there. Because appointments are time-sensitive, RealtyJuggler offers the ability to set an alarm for Appointments using the Alarm check-box. RealtyJuggler then sends you an email reminding you of the Appointment.

By default, you must check the Alarm check-box for each Appointment you want an alarm for. You can adjust this behavior in the Main:Settings:Preferences:Calendar section of RealtyJuggler, where you can set default values for new appointments.

Tasks do not have a time associated with them. They are also unique in that if you miss doing a task by the due date, the task stays around until you get it done, and its due date is shown in bold and red. This distinction is a critical one and should guide you when deciding whether to create a new task or a new Appointment.

A good habit when using RealtyJuggler is to take a quick look at both your "All Tasks" and "Calendar" sections first thing in the morning. Your Appointments in your Calendar are your highest priority, while Tasks can be fit in between your Appointments, as they are not time-sensitive. There will sometimes be overlap between the two, but the more you differentiate between them, the easier it will be to organize and prioritize your day.

Here is a specific scenario that explains the distinction:

1. A lead comes into RealtyJuggler and you immediately call the lead. They tell you they are interested but need to think about it. Can you call me in 2 weeks?
2. You enter 14 into the Contact Again in __ days field.

Every morning when you sign into RealtyJuggler you click the All Tasks icon and then the Calendar to review what you need to do for the day. If you do that, you are reminded to contact this person again in 14 days. Since this was a loose commitment on your part, it makes sense to make it a task.

Now a slightly different scenario:

1. A lead comes into RealtyJuggler and you immediately call the lead. They tell you they are interested but would like the spouse to be present. Can you call next Sunday at 11PM?
2. You click the Appointments tab inside the Prospect record. You enter the date and time of the appointment and click the blue down arrow to add the appointment to your calendar.

This time an appointment shows up in your calendar. You can synchronize this appointment with Outlook and your phone. You can even set up an email reminder so you do not miss the appointment.

The key difference between these two scenarios is the time-critical nature of the appointment compared with the loose requirements of a Task. If you have a vague commitment to call someone in a few days, that is a task, not an appointment.

## Conclusion

Tasks are an excellent way to automate your workflow. They give you a way to ensure a uniform level of service to every client. They also provide an important level of documentation that your Sellers will appreciate. For closings, tasks let you identify little problems before they become large ones. For Prospects, you can use tasks to follow up on leads even if the incubation process takes months. The result is less stress for you and a happier client.
